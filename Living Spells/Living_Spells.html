Dungeon Masters: Living Spells Play-Along Pack

Dungeon Masters: Living Spell Play-Along Pack

Introduction

For those who master the secrets of the Weave, unfettered power awaits.

This encounter is the first in the Dungeon Masters: Arcana Unleashed Play-Along Pack, a collection of stand-alone encounters inspired by episodes of D&D’s official actual play series, Dungeon Masters. It also provides a first look at monsters, magic items, and faction from Arcana Unleashed: The Evoker Archmage and Living Spells stat blocks, the evolving Idol of Good Fortunes magic item, and character options and faction information surrounding the Ninth Quill faction.

Inspiration

Inspired by the ideas that series DM Jasmine Bhullar prepared for the session, the encounters reflect preparation that occurred behind the screen for Campaign 1. As is the case in any D&D adventure, the characters may take unexpected paths or make choices that lead the story in a different direction.

The encounters in this pack have been adapted for play rather than designed to recreate exact scenes from the show. That way, you can bring them to your table no matter where your own adventures lead. We’re excited to have you roll with us.

Using This Supplement

To use this supplement, you need the D&D Beyond Basic Rules (available for free on D&D Beyond). In Appendix A and Appendix B, you’ll find the necessary stat blocks and magic items from Arcana Unleashed. Each encounter features a map from various official sources, because that’s what Jasmine used in the show.

When a creature’s name appears in bold type, that’s a visual cue pointing you to its stat block as a way of saying, “Hey, DM, you should get this creature’s stat block ready. You’re going to need it.”

Play On D&D Beyond

Bring this adventure to life with the Maps VTT, the official virtual tabletop for Dungeons & Dragons. Access it at DnDBeyond.com/games and you’ll find this quickplay adventure already set up—complete with the map, monsters, and tokens ready for your party to play. Want to make it your own? Use those same components (and any others in your collection) to forge entirely new adventures all in Maps VTT.

Living Spells

Encounter for Level 6 Characters

Situation. Magical disturbances have caused unstable arcane anomalies to manifest across the region. Resonant magical energy has begun spontaneously animating unstable spell components.

Hook. The characters are agents of the Ninth Quill faction and have infiltrated an Evoker Archmage’s workshop in pursuit of an evolving magic item, an Idol of Good Fortunes. During their search of the area, unstable magical components coalesce into living spells and attack.

The Ninth Quill Faction

The Ninth Quill are a secretive faction of thieves, saboteurs, and spies who steal dangerous magical lore and artifacts from other organizations, claiming such power is safest in their hands. They infiltrate rival factions, sabotage magical operations, and manipulate conflicts to prevent any single magical organization from becoming too powerful.

More information about the Ninth Quill can be found in Appendix C. Eight other magical factions are included in Chapter 4 of Arcana Unleashed.

Use the warehouse map below. It includes the following features.

Arcane Miscellany. Crates line the walls, each packed with spell components, alchemical reagents, and damaged magical devices. In three crates (chosen by the DM), these volatile contents have mixed and reacted with the ambient magical resonance, creating living spells within.

Living Spells. When a creature enters the warehouse, the dormant living spells begin violently straining against their containers (chosen by the DM or randomly determined).

A crate has AC 12, 15 Hit Points, Immunity to Poison and Psychic damage, and Vulnerability to Force damage. A crate reduced to 0 Hit Points ruptures, releasing the living spell within. Roll on the table below to determine which living spell emerges.

1d6 Living Spell
1 Living Flaming Sphere
2 Living Acid Arrow
3 Living Gust of Wind
4 Living Shatter
5 Living Burning Hands
6 Living Chromatic Orb

When a mundane crate is destroyed, roll on the Trinkets table or choose a piece of Adventuring Gear to determine its contents.

Conclusion. Once the living spells are destroyed, the magical resonance subsides, and the warehouse can be safely searched. The characters can recover the Idol of Good Fortunes they came for, alongside salvageable magical supplies worth 250 GP.

Evolving the Idol of Good Fortunes. You decide when a magic item evolves. In general, a magic item evolves when a character's power level increases to the next tier of play. An evolved magic item retains its properties from its previous rarity.

Appendix A: Monsters

Living Spells

Animate Magical Effects

Habitat: Any; Treasure: None

Magical energies unleashed during cataclysmic battles or catastrophic experiments can cause spell effects to come to life as living spells. These beings’ magical energy is persistent, and they bear features resembling those of their origin spells—usually Conjuration or Evocation spells. Living spells typically linger in the region of their creation, subsisting on ambient magical energy and lashing out at creatures they encounter.

Evoker Archmage

Masters of Magic

Habitat: Any; Treasure: Arcana, Individual

Although all archmages have mastered incredible magical power, some specialize in a particular school of magic, pushing their abilities to greater heights in that area. These archmages stock their spellbooks with spells from their preferred school and have developed unique magical techniques.

Evoker archmages wield unbridled destructive magic with surgical precision. They can carve out safe areas in an explosive blast, shielding themselves and allies while obliterating foes.

Appendix B: Magic Items

Idol of Good Fortunes

Wondrous Item, Rarity Varies (Requires Attunement)

This idol resembles an elephant sitting on its haunches, smiling regally behind its uplifted trunk.

Blemished (Uncommon). The idol is made of tarnished bronze, with minor dings and dents scattered over its surface.

You can cast the Augury spell from the idol. The idol speaks the omen aloud in response. Once the idol has been used to cast this spell, it can’t be used to cast this spell again until the next dawn.

Tarnished (Rare). The idol is made of gold, with a patina of dust settled into its crevices.

While attuned to the idol, you gain a +1 bonus to your Armor Class. Additionally, while holding the idol, you can take a Magic action to touch a nonmagical trade bar or gemstone. The item is then transformed into coinage equivalent to its value. For example, if you take a Magic action to touch a 5-pound silver bar while holding the idol, the bar is transformed into 25 GP worth of coins.

Golden (Very Rare). The idol is made of gleaming gold, and the elephant’s eyes are set with shining rubies.

While holding the idol, you can cast Globe of Invulnerability from it. Once the idol has been used to cast this spell, it can’t be used to cast this spell again until the next dawn.

Appendix C: Factions and Character Options

Agent of the Ninth Quill

Ability Scores: Dexterity, Intelligence, Charisma

Feat: Arcane Infiltrator

Skill Proficiencies: Arcana and Sleight of Hand

Tool Proficiency: Thieves' Tools

Equipment:Choose A or B: (A) Dagger, Light Hammer, Thieves’ Tools, Iron Spikes, Rope, Traveler’s Clothes, 17 GP; or (B) 50 GP

Your sharp mind and keen burgling skills led you to fall in with the Ninth Quill, a collective of thieves, saboteurs, and spies who steal magical lore and relics from others. Through your escapades—from purloining magic items and arcane tomes to sabotaging groups of mages—you’ve developed cunning skills and magical tricks to further your spy craft.

Arcane Infiltrator

Origin Feat

You gain the following benefits.

Cantrip. You learn the Friends cantrip. Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for this spell (choose when you select this feat).

Cunning Diversion. You can take the Dodge action as a Bonus Action. You can use this benefit a number of times equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

Faction: Ninth Quill

The Ninth Quill is a faction of thieves, saboteurs, and spies who use magic to obtain and sequester powerful artifacts and knowledge by any means necessary—including robbing and sabotaging other factions. The faction’s leaders consider their organization to be superior to factions focused on a specific school of magic and the safest custodian of dangerous lore and relics, but some members also steal valuables and undermine other organizations for personal gain.

Goals and Activities

Ninth Quill members’ adaptability and diverse areas of expertise help them infiltrate other groups, often posing as new recruits or long-absent members from other lands.

The Ninth Quill has a few popular catchphrases that members use as meaningful mantras—or excuses for criminality. These phrases are described in the sections that follow.

Our Hands Are Safest

Ninth Quill members often steal magic items and research to keep them out of others’ hands. They receive extensive training in handling dangerous artifacts and resisting the influence of sentient objects. In principle, these items are sequestered in the Ninth Quill vaults (which members insist are more secure than any other faction’s), but less scrupulous members might keep certain items for their own use.

Others Learn So We Can Earn

When the Ninth Quill steals discoveries and relics that aren’t particularly harmful, it sells them to wealthy patrons to fund its operations. Desperate buyers rebuffed by other factions, such as a necromancer rejected by the Covenant of the Grave or a transmuter suspicious of the Bringers of Cosmic Dawn, might seek the services of the Ninth Quill to obtain such objects.

Discontent Is Our Best Defense

The Ninth Quill seeks to destabilize other factions that become too powerful. This often involves agents infiltrating a faction, then undertaking whisper campaigns, sabotaging experiments, and encouraging critical personnel to depart. The Ninth Quill agents quietly slip away once they have stoked sufficient inefficiency, paranoia, and infighting.

Leadership and Organization

The Ninth Quill values intelligence, resourcefulness, and loyalty. A significant majority of Ninth Quill agents use spellcasting to enhance their spy craft.

A Careful Hierarchy

Although Ninth Quill leaders take pains to hide their identities, the faction operates under a strict hierarchal system. Agents are the lowest ranking, followed by Lieutenants, followed by Masters. Those in the highest echelon are Guild Masters.

Joining the Ninth Quill

The Ninth Quill operates in the shadows and therefore doesn’t entertain applicants. However, the Guild Masters keep an eye on talented thieves and other morally ambiguous individuals with magical skills. If the Guild Masters believe such an individual would be an asset to the Ninth Quill, they extend an invitation to visit Blackscript College— through an anonymous courier, of course.

Blackscript College

The hidden academy where the Ninth Quill trains its members is located in a crime-ridden neighborhood of a busy trade city. Dilapidated warehouses, all owned by the Ninth Quill, surround the college to ensure its privacy. There’s no readily apparent entrance; instead, it’s accessible only by unmarked doors in twisting alleys and hidden passages.

The centuries-old college was once a legitimate magical academy, but city records show that it closed decades ago due to administrative corruption. In truth, the college simply went underground to teach magical spy craft and thievery.

A former instructor at Blackscript College’s predecessor academy, the college’s dean is the elf Sirethe Slipshadow (Medium Spy Master). Slipshadow is infamous for her ability to impersonate others and uses these skills to observe students during their studies and on early missions.

The college’s library, called the epository, holds an eclectic variety of stolen treasures. An adjacent chamber called the Ersatz Repository contains worktables where members make high-quality counterfeits of spellbooks, relics, and artifacts. Many cherished treasures held by other factions are actually Ninth Quill counterfeits.

As their Renown Scores increase, characters gain access to various perks, as shown on the Ninth Quill Renown table. If your campaign doesn’t use renown, the DM decides if and when characters earn these perks.

Ninth Quill Characters

The Ninth Quill uses thieves and enforcers, but the group is no mere criminal guild; its leaders prefer recruits who have magical skill or knowledge.

The most welcome are Bards, Fighters (especially those with the Eldritch Knight subclass), Rogues (especially those with the Arcane Trickster subclass), Warlocks, or Wizards. Characters with proficiency in Arcana or Deception; the Agent of the Ninth Quill, Charlatan, or Criminal background; or the Magic Initiate feat are also valued.

If you’re a member of the Ninth Quill, you receive these benefits.

Modest Lifestyle. The Ninth Quill pays you a stipend to support your Modest lifestyle (1 GP per day).

Trinket. Roll on the Ninth Quill Trinkets table to determine what trinket you have from your time with the Ninth Quill.

Using This Faction

The Ninth Quill often opposes other magical factions. You might introduce Ninth Quill Bandits or Scouts looking to steal the characters’ hard-won items or lore, or you could establish intrigue-laden plots about Ninth Quill Spies, Spy Masters, or Bandit Deceivers who stoke rivalries and paranoia within another faction.

Alternatively, the Ninth Quill could employ or aid the characters. The faction might recruit the characters to retrieve a valuable asset or assist them with recovering and disposing of a dangerous artifact. You could even build a campaign around efforts to steal relics from each of the other magical factions.

Special Facility

Members of the Ninth Quill can add the following special facility to their Bastions.

Heist Planning Chamber

Level 9 Bastion Facility

Prerequisite: Membership in the Ninth Quill

Space: Roomy

Hirelings: 1

Order: Research

This room has a planning table, pieces of miniature terrain to represent walls or furniture, and tomes on magical security, architecture, and spy craft useful when planning a heist.

Research: Reconnaissance. When you issue the Research order to this facility, your hireling sends spies to evaluate a building within 50 miles of your Bastion. This work takes 7 days. At the end of the seventh day, the hireling provides a model of the building on the planning table. Public areas of the building are depicted accurately, and private areas are depicted only generally. Any area concealed by magic or protected by powerful guardians, at the DM’s discretion, might not be depicted accurately or at all. The spies avoid all creatures, so information about the building’s inhabitants is sparse.

The model remains until you use the Research order on this facility again, but it might become inaccurate over time if the building changes.

Heist Preparations. A character who prepares for a theft or infiltration using the model for at least 8 hours chooses one of the following roles. The role’s benefit applies while the character is within 1 mile of the building depicted by the model and lasts for 7 days or until you prepare again:

Enforcer: Advantage on Charisma (Intimidation) checks

Face: Advantage on Charisma (Deception) checks

Lookout: Advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks

Medic: Advantage on Wisdom (Medicine) checks

Sneak: Advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks

Thief: Advantage on Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) checks

Enlarging the Facility. You can enlarge your Heist Planning Chamber to a Vast facility by spending 2,000 GP. If you do so, the range of Reconnaissance increases to 500 miles, and a character preparing for a heist can choose two roles, gaining the benefits of both.
