<p class="efota-dropcap">This chapter offers new backgrounds, species, and feats. The <a href="#Khoravar">Khoravar</a> species in this chapter is a new game option, representing Eberron folk who are sometimes called half-elves. The <a href="#Changeling">Changeling</a>, <a href="#Kalashtar">Kalashtar</a>, <a href="#Shifter">Shifter</a>, and <a href="#Warforged">Warforged</a> species are new versions of species that have appeared in other books; in consultation with your DM, choose the version you prefer! The <a href="#DragonmarkFeats">Dragonmark feats</a> in this chapter offer a new way to join the dragonmarked houses.</p>

<h2 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="Dragonmarks">Dragonmarks</h2>

<div class="flexible-double-column">

  <div class="house-banner">
      <figure class="compendium-art compendium-art-center house-banner" id="imgdragonmarks">
        <span class="artist-credit">Lee Moyer</span>
        <a class="ddb-lightbox-outer compendium-image-center" href="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-005.dragonmarks.png" data-lightbox="1" data-title=""><img src="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-005.dragonmarks.png" class="ddb-lightbox-inner" style="width: 445px;" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></a>
        <figcaption>Left to Right: Mark of Detection, Mark of Shadow, Mark of Making</figcaption>
      </figure>
  </div>

  <div>
<p>Dragonmarks are mysterious and magical symbols that appear on the skin of some people across Khorvaire. The twelve established dragonmarks are tied to specific family lines, and the scions of those families work hard at consolidating the power of the marks in order to maximize the profit and influence they gain from them. The <a href="#MarkofHealing">Mark of Healing</a>, for example, has given the halflings of House Jorasco an edge in providing medical care; the house has exploited that edge to the point where it remains virtually the only provider of healing services. Anyone outside the house who manifests the Mark of Healing is quickly pressured to join, regardless of their family line or species.</p>
  </div>

</div>




<h3 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="GreaterDragonmarks">Greater Dragonmarks</h3>

<div class="flexible-double-column">

  <div class="house-banner">
      <figure class="compendium-art compendium-art-center house-banner" id="imggreaterdragonmarks">
        <span class="artist-credit">Lee Moyer</span>
        <a class="ddb-lightbox-outer compendium-image-center" href="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-006.greater-dragonmarks.png" data-lightbox="1" data-title=""><img src="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-006.greater-dragonmarks.png" class="ddb-lightbox-inner" style="width: 445px;" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></a>
        <figcaption>Left to Right: Greater Mark of Detection, Greater Mark of Shadow, Greater Mark of Making</figcaption>
      </figure>
  </div>

  <div>
<p>Most dragonmarks are small—no more than the size of the bearer’s palm—and grant limited magical power. And even these minor marks are rather rare. But rarer and more powerful marks appear on some individuals, both within and outside the dragonmarked houses. Greater dragonmarks manifest only on people who already possess an ordinary mark. When a greater dragonmark manifests, it grows from an ordinary mark and expands outward to two or three times the original mark’s size.</p>
<p>The dragonmarked houses view greater marks as more significant than ordinary marks in every way—more important to the livelihood of the house, more essential to keep under the house’s control, and potentially more significant in terms of fulfilling the Draconic Prophecy that some believe is written in the marks.</p>
  </div>

</div>



<h3 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="SiberysDragonmarks">Siberys Dragonmarks</h3>

<div class="flexible-double-column">

  <div class="house-banner">
      <figure class="compendium-art compendium-art-center" id="imgSiberysDragonmarks">
        <span class="artist-credit">Lee Moyer</span>
        <a class="ddb-lightbox-outer compendium-image-center" href="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-007.siberys-dragonmarks.png" data-lightbox="1" data-title=""><img src="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-007.siberys-dragonmarks.png" class="ddb-lightbox-inner" style="width: 445px;" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></a>
        <figcaption>Left to Right: Siberys Mark of Detection, Siberys Mark of Shadow, Siberys Mark of Making</figcaption>
      </figure>
  </div>

  <div>
<p>While the people of Khorvaire have a limited understanding of dragonmarks, Siberys dragonmarks remain a complete mystery to them. The magic of these powerful marks clearly relates—visually and thematically—to the other marks in one’s house. But a dragonmarked heir may spend years perfecting and mastering the powers of a mark without ever manifesting a Siberys mark. Meanwhile, an unmarked scion of a dragonmarked house might suddenly display one against all expectations.</p>
<p>Siberys marks are mighty, unpredictable, and rare, and the dragonmarked houses keep a careful eye on anyone who manifests one. If its power can serve the house, fine—but an heir of Siberys who doesn’t conceal the dragonmark’s ability or remain firmly aligned with the dragonmarked house is likely to become the target of assassins.</p>
  </div>

</div>


<p>The <a href="#BoonofSiberys">Boon of Siberys</a>, described in the “<a href="#Feats">Feats</a>” section later in this chapter, can reflect either a Siberys mark or a powerful aberrant mark. Alternatively, at a player’s discretion and with the DM’s approval, a character’s acquisition of a different Epic Boon feat can represent the manifestation of a Siberys mark. A character with ties to House Lyrandar, for example, might choose the Boon of Energy Resistance, gaining Resistance to Lightning and Thunder damage, and manifest the Siberys mark of Storm. A character with ties to House Phiarlan or House Thuranni might represent the Siberys mark of Shadow by choosing the Boon of the Night Spirit.</p>


<aside class="text--rules-sidebar efota--rules-sidebar" id="WhatsNewDragonmarks">
    <p>What’s New with Dragonmarks?</p>
    <p>Compared to the rules in <a class="sourcebook" href="https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/erftlw">Eberron: Rising from the Last War</a>, here’s what’s different about dragonmarks in this chapter:</p>
    <ul>
            <li>The benefits of each dragonmark now derive from feats rather than species options. These feats belong to a special category, Dragonmark feats.</li>
            <li>Dragonmark magic works a little differently than before: now you can use any spell slots you have to cast the spells your dragonmark grants you. In a few cases, this change called for adjustments to the Spells of the Mark feature of a dragonmark; see the “<a href="#DragonmarkFeats">Dragonmark Feats</a>” section of this chapter.</li>
            <li>In addition to the Dragonmark feats, thirteen new General feats represent greater dragonmarks, and one more increases a mark’s potency. And one new entry in the Epic Boon category, the Boon of Siberys, can represent either a powerful aberrant dragonmark or a Siberys mark.</li>
            <li>A character of any species can take a Dragonmark feat and manifest a dragonmark.</li>
            <li>In the <a class="sourcebook" href="https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024">Player’s Handbook (2024)</a>, anyone who has a Ritual spell prepared can cast that spell as a Ritual. This rule means the Ritual spells granted by Dragonmark feats can now be cast as Rituals by anyone who has those feats.</li>
    </ul>
</aside>


<h3 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="MarksofMystery">Marks of Mystery</h3>

<p>Centuries of manipulation and consolidation have caused the established dragonmarks to manifest primarily in members of certain family lines. However, exceptions have always existed, and they’ve grown increasingly common in recent years.</p>
<p>In <a class="sourcebook" href="https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/erftlw">Eberron: Rising from the Last War</a>, only a character of a particular species could have each dragonmark. Those rules reflected the common perception that dragonmarks run only in family lines. This book instead allows a character of any species to manifest any dragonmark.</p>
<p>If you choose a dragonmark for your character, consider these four options for its origin.</p>
  <hr class="separator">

<h4 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="DragonmarkedHeir">Dragonmarked Heir</h4>

<p>You are a member of a dragonmarked house and its family line—for example, if you bear the <a href="#MarkofHealing">Mark of Healing</a>, you could be a Halfling member of House Jorasco and related to the other dragonmarked heirs of the house. In this case, you have much in common with the NPC heirs of your house. The actions of your house influence you, although you and your DM can determine to what extent you become drawn into house politics.</p>
<p>Taking the background associated with your house (such as <a href="#HouseJorascoHeir">House Jorasco Heir</a>) is the best way to build your character as a dragonmarked heir. Alternatively, you can take the <a href="#HouseAgent">House Agent</a> background, or some other background reflecting your character’s history, and choose a Dragonmark feat later in your career.</p>
<p>Since dragonmarks typically appear for the first time in situations of stress or peril where the power of the mark could prove useful, heirs of the dragonmarked houses undergo a rite of passage called the Test of Siberys in adolescence. Each house’s test is different, but they all involve some danger designed to evoke the power of the house’s mark. Usually, a dragonmark appears during the test, if it ever manifests at all, but heirs sometimes manifest their marks years after failing the Test of Siberys.</p>
  <hr class="separator">

<h4 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="DistantOffshoot">Distant Offshoot</h4>

<p>You are a distant offshoot of a dragonmarked family line, sharing the species that most commonly manifests the mark but unencumbered by ties to its house. For example, you could be a Halfling with the <a href="#MarkofHealing">Mark of Healing</a> but with no ties to House Jorasco. You’re an anomaly; if House Jorasco became aware of you, it would try to bring you and your family line into the house. To build this kind of character, choose a Dragonmark feat at level 4 or later.</p>
  <hr class="separator">

<h4 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="MarkofProphechy">Mark of Prophecy</h4>

<p>You might bear a mark unrelated to your species. For example, you can choose to bear the <a href="#MarkofHealing">Mark of Healing</a> even if you’re not a Halfling. Your dragonmark has nothing to do with the house’s family line. It might be a direct mark of the Draconic Prophecy, or it could be an aberrant mark that happens to duplicate an established one. You’ll probably never meet an NPC in the world like you, and House Jorasco might not claim you.</p>
<p>To build a character with a mark like this, choose a Dragonmark feat at level 4 or later or select a house background (or the Aberrant Heir background). With your DM’s permission, you can alter some of the background’s proficiencies to better reflect your character’s origin.</p>
  <hr class="separator">

<h4 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="AberrantMark">Aberrant Mark</h4>

<p>You bear an aberrant dragonmark. These rare marks appear on members of all species. Aberrant dragonmarks disturb the houses—you would be wise to avoid their attention. The <a href="#AberrantHeir">Aberrant Heir</a> background is the best way to build a character with an aberrant mark. Alternatively, choose another background and take the Aberrant Dragonmark feat at level 4 or later.</p>

<h2 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="Backgrounds">Backgrounds</h2>

<p>This section presents seventeen backgrounds using the same format as those in the <a class="sourcebook" href="https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024">Player’s Handbook</a>. The fourteen that represent dragonmarked heirs offer Dragonmark feats rather than Origin feats; the Dragonmark feats appear at the end of the chapter. Choosing one of these backgrounds is the only way to acquire a Dragonmark feat at level 1; characters who select a different background can choose Dragonmark feats anytime they have an unrestricted feat choice. The <a href="#HouseAgent">House Agent</a> background grants house affiliation without a dragonmark.</p>
<p>In addition to the backgrounds connected to dragonmarks and their houses, the <a href="#Archaeologist">Archaeologist</a> and <a href="#Inquisitive">Inquisitive</a> backgrounds support the campaigns presented in this book (see chapters <a href="/chapters/eberron-gazetteer/">4</a> and <a href="/chapters/friends-and-foes/">6</a>).</p>

<h3 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="Changeling">Changeling</h3>

<p>With ever-changing appearances, changelings reside in many societies undetected. Each changeling can supernaturally adopt any face they like. For some changelings, a new face may reveal an aspect of their soul.</p>
<p>The first changelings in the multiverse appeared in the Feywild, and the wondrous, mutable essence of that plane lingers in changelings today—even in those who have never set foot in the fey realm.</p>
<p>In their true form, changelings appear faded, their features almost devoid of detail. It is rare to see one in that form, for a typical changeling alters their shape the way others might change clothes. However, many changelings develop identities with more depth, crafting a persona complete with history and beliefs to go with each shape. A changeling adventurer might have personas for many situations, including negotiation, investigation, and combat.</p>
<p>Multiple changelings can share a persona. Personas can even pass down through a family, allowing a younger changeling to take advantage of contacts established by the persona’s previous users.</p>

<p>You stay in the new form until you take an action to revert to your true form.</p>

<h3 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="Shifter">Shifter</h3>

<p>Shifters—sometimes called “weretouched”—descend from people who contracted full or partial lycanthropy. Humanoids with a bestial aspect, shifters can’t change shape fully, but they can enhance their animalistic features temporarily in a process they call “shifting.”</p>
<p>Shifters resemble humans in height and build but are typically more lithe and flexible. Their facial features have a bestial cast, often with large eyes and pointed ears; most shifters also possess prominent canine teeth. They grow fur-like hair on nearly every part of their bodies. While a shifter’s appearance might remind an onlooker of an animal, the shifter remains clearly identifiable as a Humanoid even when at their most feral.</p>

<p><strong>Swiftstride.</strong> While you are shifted, your Speed increases by 10 feet. Additionally, you can move up to 10 feet as a Reaction when a creature ends its turn within 5 feet of you. This reactive movement doesn’t provoke Opportunity Attack action.</p>
<p><strong>Wildhunt.</strong> While shifted, you have Advantage on Wisdom checks.     <a class="ddb-lightbox-outer compendium-image-center" href="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-031.Species-Warforged.png" data-lightbox="1" data-title=""><img src="https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/efota/Jae5SyNcfFGjapTb/02-031.Species-Warforged.png" class="ddb-lightbox-inner compendium-center-banner-img" style="width: 850px;" alt="Species Warforged" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></a>
    <span class="artist-credit">Ignacio Bazan-Lazcano</span>
  </figure>
<p>Warforged are mechanical beings built as weapons to fight in the Last War. An unexpected breakthrough produced sentient beings made from wood and metal that nevertheless can feel pain and emotion.</p>
<p>Warforged comprise a blend of organic and inorganic materials. Rootlike cords infused with alchemical fluids serve as their muscles, wrapped around a framework of steel, darkwood, or stone. Armored plates form a protective outer shell and reinforce joints. The more a warforged cultivates their individuality, the more likely they are to modify their body, seeking out an artificer to customize the look of their face, limbs, and plating.</p>
  <hr class="separator">

<h4 class="compendium-hr heading-anchor" id="WarforgedTraits">Warforged Traits</h4>

<div class="condensed-group">
    <p><strong>Creature Type:</strong> Construct</p>
    <p><strong>Size:</strong> Medium (about 6–8 feet tall) or Small (about 3–4 feet tall), chosen when you select this species</p>
    <p><strong>Speed:</strong> 30 feet</p>
    <p>As a Warforged, you have these special traits.</p>
</div>

<p><strong><em> Construct Resilience. </em></strong> You have Resistance to Poison damage. You also have Advantage on saving throws to avoid or end the <a class="tooltip-hover condition-tooltip" href="https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#PoisonedCondition" data-tooltip-href="/conditions/11-tooltip">Poisoned</a> condition.</p>
<p><strong><em> Integrated Protection. </em></strong> You gain a +1 bonus to your Armor Class. In addition, armor you have donned can’t be removed against your will while you’re alive.</p>
<p><strong><em> Sentry’s Rest. </em></strong> You don’t need to sleep, and magic can’t put you to sleep. You can finish a Long Rest in 6 hours if you spend those hours in an inactive, motionless state. During this time, you appear inert but remain conscious.</p>
<p><strong><em> Specialized Design. </em></strong> You gain one skill proficiency and one tool proficiency of your choice.</p>
<p><strong><em> Tireless. </em></strong> You don’t gain <a class="tooltip-hover condition-tooltip" href="https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#ExhaustionCondition" data-tooltip-href="/conditions/4-tooltip">Exhaustion</a> levels from dehydration, malnutrition, or suffocation.</p>

<h4 class="compendium-hr with-metadata heading-anchor" id="AberrantDragonmark">Aberrant Dragonmark</h4>

<p><em>Dragonmark Feat (Prerequisite: Eberron Campaign, Can’t Have Another Dragonmark Feat)</em></p>
<p>You gain the following benefits.</p>
<p><strong><em> Aberrant Fortitude. </em></strong> When you fail a Constitution saving throw, you can take a Reaction to roll 1d4 and add the number rolled to the save, potentially turning the failure into a success. Once you’ve used this benefit, you can’t use it again until you finish a Long Rest.</p>
<p><strong><em> Aberrant Magic. </em></strong> You know one cantrip of your choice from the Sorcerer spell list. Also, choose a level 1 spell from that spell list. You always have that spell prepared. You can cast it once without a spell slot, and you regain the ability to cast it in that way when you finish a Short or Long Rest. You can also cast this spell using any spell slots you have. Constitution is your spellcasting ability for this spell.</p>
<p><strong><em> Aberrant Surge. </em></strong> When you cast the level 1 spell from this feat, you can expend one of your Hit Point Dice and roll it. If you roll an even number, you gain a number of Temporary Hit Points equal to the number rolled. If you roll an odd number, one creature within 30 feet of you (not including you) takes Force damage equal to the number rolled. If no other creatures are in range, you take the damage.</p>
